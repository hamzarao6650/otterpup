How Pup reads a plan

Discharge plans are dense, full of abbreviations and sometimes handwritten. Pup uses AI to do four jobs in order.

Read

The patient or a family member sends a photo or PDF of the plan. Pup reads printed and handwritten text from the image.

Understand

Pup finds every medicine, dose and timing, how to take it, the exercises and each follow-up visit, and decodes shorthand like "PO BID" into plain words.

Plan

Pup lays the instructions out as small daily steps, so the patient sees exactly what to do this morning, this afternoon and tonight.

Remind and answer

Reminders arrive on WhatsApp, visits land on the calendar, and Pup answers questions using the patient's own plan.

Built to be safe

Pup helps patients follow the plan their care team already wrote. It never replaces the care team.

Follows the plan, never changes it

Pup does not diagnose, treat, or change any dose or instruction. It explains and reminds.

Answers from the patient's own plan

Questions are answered from the plan the patient sent. Anything else goes back to the care team.

Knows when to step back

For worrying symptoms, Pup points patients to their care team, and in an emergency, to 911.

Keeps messages light

WhatsApp messages carry reminders and plain-language steps, not diagnoses or test results.

HIPAA-ready before hospitals

Hospital pilots will run only on HIPAA-eligible services covered by business associate agreements.

Clear about what exists

OtterPup is an early prototype. We label planned features as planned, everywhere on this site.

What a first hospital pilot could look like

We are looking for a first hospital or clinic partner. This is the shape of pilot we are proposing.

  1. Pick one unitStart with one discharging unit, such as orthopedics or cardiology, where plans are long and follow-ups matter.
  2. Enroll at dischargePatients who opt in send their plan to Pup before they leave, so day one starts on time.
  3. Call who needs helpThe care team sees missed doses and unbooked follow-ups and calls only those patients. Dashboard planned
  4. Measure what mattersCompare missed doses, follow-up attendance and 30-day readmissions with the unit's usual numbers.

A proposed plan, not a result. Pilot terms will be shaped with our first partner.
