How OtterPup works

Patients send their plan once. Pup does the rest, on the app they already use every day.

  1. Send the plan

    Upload a photo or PDF of the discharge or care plan, even if parts are handwritten.

  2. Pup reads it

    Pup pulls out each medicine and when to take it, how to take it (with food, with water, by mouth), the exercises and the follow-up visits, and rewrites them as small daily steps in plain language.

  3. Reminders arrive on WhatsApp

    At each dose, Pup sends a short message with the medicine and how to take it. The app sends a notification too.

  4. Appointments land on the calendar

    Follow-ups become Google Calendar invites with the doctor's name and a map link, plus reminders the day before and one to two hours before.

  5. Ask Pup anything about the plan

    Pup answers questions from the patient's own plan and points them back to their care team for anything else.

Patients use it free. Hospitals see who needs a call.

OtterPup is built to be paid for by hospitals and clinics that want fewer missed doses and fewer avoidable readmissions.

  • No setup needed for patients to start
  • A dashboard of missed doses, exercises and follow-ups for every enrolled patient

Common questions

Does OtterPup give medical advice?

No. Pup helps patients follow the plan their care team already gave them. It does not diagnose, treat or change any instructions. For a medical question, patients should contact their care team, and in an emergency, call 911.

Do patients need a new app?

No. Reminders and answers come through WhatsApp, which many patients and family members already use every day.

Is OtterPup HIPAA compliant?

Not yet. OtterPup is a prototype. A HIPAA-ready setup is planned before any hospital pilot.

What does it cost?

Patients use OtterPup free. The plan is for hospitals and clinics to pay for the patients they enroll.
